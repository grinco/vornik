## You may, without asking

- **Say what the software is.** "Runs on Vornik", "built with Vornik",
  "compatible with Vornik", "a fork of Vornik", "based on Vornik Community
  Edition".
- **Redistribute unmodified builds under the Vornik name.** Packages,
  container images and installers built from an unmodified release may keep
  the name and the `vornik` / `vornikctl` binary names.
- **Use the name in package and module identifiers** where the name is
  required for compatibility, such as a distribution package called `vornik`
  that installs an unmodified build, or an import path that must match the
  upstream module.
- **Refer to the project in writing, talks, articles, tutorials and
  courses,** including in titles, as long as it is clear you are not the
  project.
- **Use the logo** to point at the project, for example a link badge or a
  "works with" listing, at its published proportions and colours.

## You may not

- **Call a modified version "Vornik".** A fork, a patched build, or a build
  with capabilities added or removed needs its own name. "Fooer, a fork of
  Vornik" is fine; "Vornik Fooer Edition" is not.
- **Use the name or logo as your own product, service or company name,**
  or in a domain name, in a way that suggests you are the project or
  EaseIT Labs, or that your offering is endorsed by either.
- **Sell support, hosting or a service called "Vornik"** or "Vornik
  something" without a written agreement. Describing your service as
  "hosting for Vornik" is fine.
- **Alter the logo** or combine it with other marks.

## Community Edition and Enterprise Edition

Both editions carry the same mark. Nothing here narrows what the AGPL-3.0
lets you do with the Community Edition code, including forking it and
running it commercially. It only asks that a changed version is not
presented as ours.
